Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Standard in a commercial application?

Yes. Standard code is MIT-licensed and can be used in commercial applications subject to its license terms. It does not require a Premium subscription or license key.

How do I get a Premium license key?

Submit a pricing or access request with your email, use case, and deployment origins. We will respond by email to confirm scope and terms before a key is issued. Do not include passwords or existing license keys in your request.

Does Premium continue working after my subscription expires?

No. This is a renewable subscription, not a perpetual Premium license. Premium access stops at subscription expiry or when its verified short-lived access lease expires. Renew the subscription, refresh the license session, and recreate disposed Premium controllers. Standard features are unaffected.

Integration and renewal lifecycle →
Do I need a new license for each package version?

No. Premium access is subscription-based, not tied to a package release. You need an active monthly or yearly subscription for the registered application origin, not a new license whenever the package is updated. Upgrading does not extend the paid subscription period.

Does a license key work on any domain or offline?

No. Premium verification checks the package, registered application origin, subscription period, and package-wide Premium access. A fresh session requires online verification; a cached short-lived lease does not grant permanent offline access. Tell us every production, staging, and local-development origin you need.

Is a hosted data backend included?

No. The package provides UI components and integration APIs. Your application owns its data services, hosting, authentication, authorization, and infrastructure costs. A package license key does not replace your application's user permissions.

What are the prices, renewal terms, and support commitments?

Contact us for a quote and choose monthly or yearly billing. Premium access follows the paid subscription period, rather than a one-time purchase of a package version. Availability, included capabilities, deployment scope, renewal pricing, support, and redistribution rights must be confirmed in the agreed terms. Sending a request does not create a subscription or authorize a payment.

What happens to previously published MIT releases?

Previously published MIT releases retain their original terms. The Standard/Premium distinction and subscription requirements described here apply to the corresponding licensed release; they do not retroactively change earlier licenses.
